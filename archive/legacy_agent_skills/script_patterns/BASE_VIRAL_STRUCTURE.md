# Base Viral Structure (Universal)

## The Three Pillars

Every Skulsy script, regardless of pattern, MUST have:

### 1. VIRAL HOOK (0-3 seconds)
**Purpose**: Stop the scroll, create curiosity

**Hook Types** (choose ONE per script):
- **Number Shock**: Specific amount that's surprising
  - "What if Skulsy put $100 into Bitcoin in 2010?"
  - "Skulsy lost $50,000 in 24 hours"
  
- **Relatable Mistake**: Common error viewers recognize
  - "Skulsy panic-sold at the worst moment"
  - "Skulsy bought high, sold low"
  
- **Unexpected Contrast**: Opposite of expectation
  - "Skulsy chose the 'safe' option"
  - "Skulsy did everything right. Still lost money."
  
- **Curiosity Gap**: Withhold key information
  - "Skulsy made one decision in 2012"
  - "Skulsy ignored everyone's advice"

**Hook Formula**:
```
[Subject: Skulsy] + [Action: specific verb] + [Object: specific number/asset/outcome]
```

**Quality Check**:
- ✅ Contains specific number OR specific mistake
- ✅ Raises a question in viewer's mind
- ✅ Under 12 words
- ❌ Vague ("Skulsy made an investment")
- ❌ Generic ("Skulsy bought stock")

---

### 2. BODY (3-40 seconds)
**Purpose**: Satisfy curiosity with story progression

**Structure Requirements**:
- **Information Density**: New fact every 5-7 seconds
- **Surprise Cadence**: Pattern break every 10-12 seconds
- **Timeline Progression**: 3+ time anchors showing movement
- **Stakes Escalation**: Each beat raises the stakes

**Body Beats** (flexible based on pattern):
1. **Setup** (3-8s): Establish context
2. **Development** (8-25s): Events unfold
3. **Consequence** (25-40s): Impact revealed

**Micro-Surprises** (Insert 3-4 per script):
- Unexpected numbers ("dropped 80%, not 20%")
- Ironic timing ("sold the day before...")
- Contrast flips ("thought he was safe, wasn't")
- Scale shifts ("$100 became $100,000")

**Quality Check**:
- ✅ Never boring for more than 5 seconds
- ✅ Each sentence advances the story
- ✅ Viewer learns new info regularly
- ❌ Repetitive phrasing
- ❌ Filler sentences

---

### 3. CLIMAX (40-45 seconds)
**Purpose**: Answer the hook's question, deliver impact

**Climax Types**:
- **The Reveal**: Final outcome number
  - "His $1,000 became $1.2 million"
  
- **The Callback**: Reference hook with new context
  - Hook: "Skulsy bought at the peak"
  - Climax: "He sold at the bottom"
  
- **The Twist**: Unexpected final detail
  - "He would've been a millionaire. If he'd waited one more day."
  
- **The Understatement**: Dark humor via minimalism
  - "He lost everything. Kept the receipt."

**Climax Formula**:
```
[Callback to hook] + [Final number/outcome] + [Understated reaction]
```

**Quality Check**:
- ✅ Directly answers hook's implicit question
- ✅ Contains most impactful number
- ✅ Feels inevitable in hindsight
- ✅ Leaves viewer with clear takeaway
- ❌ Anticlimactic
- ❌ Preachy ("so remember...")

---

## Universal Retention Mechanics

These work across ALL patterns:

### Curiosity Gaps
- Hook creates question
- Body delays answer
- Climax delivers answer
- Gap duration: 35-40 seconds

### Pattern Breaks
- Every 8-12 seconds: subvert expectation
- "He waited. He waited. He sold." (rhythm break)
- "Up 50%. Up 80%. Down 99%." (direction flip)

### Emotional Arc
- Start: Curiosity (0.5-0.6 intensity)
- Middle: Build (0.6-0.8)
- Peak: Impact (0.9-1.0)
- End: Resolution (0.7-0.8)

### Specificity
- Vague = forgettable
- Specific = memorable
- "He lost money" ❌
- "He lost $47,329" ✅
