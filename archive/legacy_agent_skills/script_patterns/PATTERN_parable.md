# Script Pattern: The Parable

**Inherits**: BASE_VIRAL_STRUCTURE.md

**Pattern Type**: Story-Driven Business Lesson (Mindlander-Style)

**Emotional Arc**: Curiosity → Skepticism → Progressive Revelation → Awe → Insight

---

## Pattern Overview

Skulsy encounters a real-world business or money problem. Through dialogue with another character, he reveals (or learns) a multi-step system that transforms the situation. The business principle is EARNED through the story, never stated upfront.

**Core Question**: "How does he turn this around?"

**Viewer Psychology**: Problem-solving fascination + "I never thought of that" + desire to feel smarter

---

## What Makes This Pattern Different

| Feature | Other Patterns | Parable |
|---------|---------------|---------|
| Voice | 100% narration | **40-60% dialogue** |
| Characters | Skulsy alone | **Skulsy + second character** |
| Structure | Linear timeline | **Progressive steps** (2-3 reveals) |
| Math | Evidence for known outcome | **Math-as-magic-trick** (surprise calculation) |
| Lesson | None (deadpan ending) | **One-line principle** at end |
| Length | 200-250 words (45s) | **350-500 words (90-120s)** |

---

## Pattern Structure (90-120 seconds)

### Beat 1: The Encounter (0-12s)

**Goal**: Skulsy walks into a situation. Establish setting + problem in 2-3 sentences.

**Tone**: Neutral, observational

**Structure**:
1. Scene-setting (one line): "Skulsy walked into a [PLACE]"
2. Problem introduction via dialogue: "[Character] said, '[PROBLEM].'"

**Example**:
```
Skulsy walked into a struggling pizza shop.
The owner said, "I sell 80 slices a day at $3. My rent is $4,000. I'm bleeding money."
```

**State**: `"hook"`
**Intensity**: 0.5-0.6
**Requirements**:
- Concrete setting (not abstract)
- Problem stated with EXACT numbers (cost, revenue, loss)
- Second character introduced naturally
- Under 30 words

---

### Beat 2: The Skeptic Moment (12-20s)

**Goal**: The second character (or Skulsy) expresses doubt. This voices the VIEWER's objection.

**Tone**: Resistance, hesitation

**Structure**:
1. Skulsy proposes something unusual
2. The other character objects: "That's crazy / I'll go bankrupt / That makes no sense"

**Example**:
```
Skulsy said, "Give away your first slice free every morning."
The owner laughed. "Free? I'm already losing money."
Skulsy said, "Who eats just one slice of pizza?"
```

**State**: `"setup"`
**Intensity**: 0.6
**Retention Trigger**: "objection" (viewer thinks the same thing, stays to hear the answer)

**Requirements**:
- Objection must be logical and relatable
- Skulsy's counter must be ONE line (not a lecture)
- The "aha" is planted but not explained yet

---

### Beat 3: The System — Progressive Steps (20-60s)

**Goal**: Reveal 2-3 strategic steps. Each one is a mini-surprise.

**Tone**: Building momentum, each step more clever than the last

**Structure**:
```
Step 1: Simple move (foot traffic / attention)
Step 2: Conversion system (membership / repeat visits)
Step 3: Multiplier (viral loop / upsell / event)
```

**Example**:
```
"Step one," Skulsy said. "Put a sign outside: First slice free before 9 AM.
Nobody eats one slice. They'll buy 2 more. That's $6 per person, not zero."

"Step two. Launch a lunch pass. $29 a month, unlimited slices between 11 and 2.
People don't eat lunch alone. They bring coworkers."

"Step three. Friday night pizza tournament.
$10 entry, eat-as-much-as-you-can. Winner gets free pizza for a month.
Post it on social media. Let the crowd do your advertising."
```

**State**: `"analysis"` → `"insight"`
**Intensity**: 0.7 → 0.8 (rising with each step)
**Retention Trigger**: "progressive_reveal" (each step creates a new "wait, there's MORE?")

**Requirements**:
- Exactly 2-3 steps (never 1, never 4+)
- Each step must include SPECIFIC numbers
- Steps must build on each other (not independent)
- Use dialogue format ("Skulsy said, '...'")
- Second character can react between steps

---

### Beat 4: The Math Payoff (60-85s)

**Goal**: Calculate the exact numbers. This is the "magic trick" moment.

**Tone**: Precise, almost clinical. Let the math shock the viewer.

**Structure**:
1. List inputs: "X customers × $Y = $Z"
2. Subtract costs: "Minus $A for [cost]"
3. Reveal profit: "$B stays in your pocket"

**Example**:
```
Skulsy did the math.
"Free slices cost you $1 each. 50 people try it. That's $50.
But 40 of them buy 2 more slices. That's $240.
Lunch passes: 100 members × $29 = $2,900 guaranteed monthly.
Friday tournament: 60 people × $10 = $600 entry, plus they buy drinks.
Total monthly: $50 + $240 × 30 + $2,900 + $2,400 = $12,500.
Rent is $4,000. Profit: $8,500."

The owner stared. "That's more than I made all last year."
```

**State**: `"climax_setup"` → `"consequence"`
**Intensity**: 0.9-1.0 (peak)
**Retention Trigger**: "math_reveal"

**Requirements**:
- ALL numbers must be traceable (no pulled-from-thin-air figures)
- Show the multiplication/addition (viewer must follow the math)
- Final number must be dramatically larger than the starting problem
- Second character's reaction sells the impact

---

### Beat 5: The Principle (85-95s)

**Goal**: One-line business truth that EARNS its weight through the story.

**Tone**: Quiet, matter-of-fact. Not preachy.

**Structure**:
1. Brief setup: "[Character] asked, 'How do you think like this?'"
2. One-line principle: "Skulsy said, '[PRINCIPLE].'"

**Example**:
```
The owner said, "How do you see things I don't?"
Skulsy said, "You were selling pizza. I was selling a system."
```

**State**: `"punchline"`
**Intensity**: 0.8
**Retention Trigger**: "principle"

**Requirements**:
- Under 15 words
- NOT "you should..." or advice
- Reframes the entire story (viewer re-evaluates everything)
- Sounds like something a mentor would actually say
- NO CTA ("follow us", "subscribe", etc.)

## Character Rules

### Skulsy in Parable Mode

Skulsy can play TWO roles:

**Role A — The Mentor** (like Mindlander's billionaire/Thomas):
- Calm, analytical
- Speaks in steps and systems
- Never brags, just calculates
- Uses questions to guide: "Who eats just one slice?"

**Role B — The Student** (less common):
- Skulsy encounters a mentor figure
- Asks the viewer's questions
- Grows through the story

### Second Character Types

| Character | Role | Examples |
|-----------|------|----------|
| **Business Owner** | Has the problem | Pizza shop, clothes seller, gym owner |
| **Skeptic** | Doubts the solution | "Free? I'll go bankrupt" |
| **Partner** | Needs convincing | Girlfriend, business partner |
| **Expert** | Teaches Skulsy | Retired investor, old mentor |

**Rule**: The second character MUST voice at least ONE objection that the viewer would naturally have.

## Dialogue Formatting

In `script.json`, dialogue is embedded in the `text` field:

```json
{
  "text": "Skulsy said, \"Give away your first slice free.\" The owner laughed. \"Free? I'll go bankrupt.\"",
  "state": "setup",
  "speaker_tags": ["skulsy", "owner"]
}
```

The `speaker_tags` array tells the TTS engine who is speaking (for future voice differentiation).

## Pattern-Specific Quality Metrics

### Dialogue Quality (0-100)
- At least 40% of text is in quotes? (+40)
- Two distinct speakers? (+30)
- Objection voiced and answered? (+30)

### Progressive Revelation (0-100)
- 2-3 distinct steps? (+40)
- Each step has specific numbers? (+30)
- Steps build on each other? (+30)

### Math Clarity (0-100)
- Calculation shown step by step? (+40)
- Final number is 3x+ the starting problem? (+30)
- All numbers traceable to story setup? (+30)

**Passing**: 75+ on each metric

## Research Integration Requirements

**MANDATORY**:
- Realistic business costs for the scenario
- Realistic customer numbers / conversion rates
- Market-appropriate pricing

**RECOMMENDED**:
- Real-world precedent (a business that actually did this)
- Industry average margins for comparison

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Skulsy Lectures Instead of Dialogues
❌ "Skulsy explained that free marketing drives foot traffic which converts to..."
✅ "Skulsy said, 'Give them free soda. Who walks past free soda?'"

### Mistake 2: Unrealistic Math
❌ "100,000 customers in the first week" (impossible for a small shop)
✅ "50 people try the free slice. 40 buy more." (realistic conversion)

### Mistake 3: No Skeptic Moment
❌ Character just agrees with everything
✅ Character says "That's insane" → Skulsy explains → Character is convinced

### Mistake 4: Steps Are Independent
❌ Step 1 has nothing to do with Step 2
✅ Step 1 creates foot traffic → Step 2 converts them to members → Step 3 makes members bring friends

### Mistake 5: Preachy Ending
❌ "So remember, in business you always need to think about systems"
✅ "You were selling pizza. I was selling a system."
