# Script Pattern: The Explainer

**Inherits**: BASE_VIRAL_STRUCTURE.md

**Pattern Type**: Confusion → Learning → Understanding → Application

**Emotional Arc**: Ignorance → Discovery → Insight → (Often) Misapplication

---

## Pattern Overview

Skulsy doesn't understand a financial concept. He learns it through experience (often the hard way). Viewer learns alongside him.

**Core Question**: "What does [CONCEPT] actually mean?"

**Viewer Psychology**: Education + entertainment + "I didn't know that either"

---

## Pattern Structure (45 seconds)

### Beat 1: The Confusion (0-8s)

**Hook Formula** (Curiosity Gap):
"Skulsy didn't know what [CONCEPT] meant"

**Examples**:
- "Skulsy didn't know what compound interest meant"
- "Skulsy had never heard of a margin call"
- "Skulsy thought 'diversification' meant owning 3 stocks"

**State**: `"hook"`  
**Intensity**: 0.5  
**Timeline Anchor**: When he learned

---

### Beat 2: The Learning (8-25s)

**Goal**: Explain concept through Skulsy's experience

**Pattern**: SHOW, don't tell

**Structure**:
1. He tries something
2. Result surprises him
3. He realizes the pattern
4. Concept clicks

**Example (Compound Interest)**:
```
"He put $1,000 in savings. 5% interest.
Year 1: $50.
He calculated: $50 every year forever.
Simple.
Year 2 wasn't $50. It was $51.25.
Year 3: $53.78.
Wait.
By year 20: $1,653. Not $2,000.
The interest earned interest."
```

**State**: `"discovery"` → `"insight"`  
**Intensity**: 0.6-0.7  
**Retention Trigger**: "surprise" (the aha moment)

---

### Beat 3: The Understanding (25-35s)

**Goal**: Crystallize the concept

**Pattern**: State the realization clearly

**Example**:
"He understood. Time makes money multiply. Not add."

**State**: `"insight"`  
**Intensity**: 0.7  
**Visual**: Light bulb moment

---

### Beat 4: The Application (35-45s)

**Punchline Types**:

**Option A - Correct Application**:
"He moved everything to a 7% account. Let it sit for 30 years."

**Option B - Wrong Application** (common):
"He understood. Moved to a 0.01% account. Safer, he thought."

**Option C - Overcorrection**:
"He got it. Put everything in a 12% crypto savings. It wasn't FDIC insured."

**State**: `"punchline"`  
**Intensity**: 0.75-0.85  
**Retention Trigger**: "twist" or "callback"

---

## Pattern-Specific Mechanics

### The Concrete Example

Never explain abstractly. Always through specific numbers.

❌ BAD:
"Compound interest makes your money grow exponentially"

✅ GOOD:
"$1,000 at 5% becomes $1,628 in 10 years. Not $1,500."

### The Relatable Misconception

Start with what MOST people think (incorrectly).

**Common Misconceptions to Address**:
- Compound interest is linear
- Diversification = multiple stocks in same sector
- High yield = low risk
- Options are "cheap" because low price
- Dollar cost averaging always wins

### The Learning Curve

Show his thought process, not just results.

**Pattern**:
1. Initial assumption: "He thought..."
2. Test: "He tried..."
3. Surprise: "But..."
4. Realization: "He understood..."

---

## Voice Guidelines

### Explain Through Experience, Not Lectures

❌ BAD:
"Compound interest is when you earn interest on interest, leading to exponential growth..."

✅ GOOD:
"Year 1: $50. Year 2: $51.25. Year 20: $653. The interest earned interest."

### Keep Skulsy as Student, Not Teacher

Skulsy learns. Viewer learns alongside. Neither teaches the other.

---

## Visual Direction

- Beat 1: Skulsy confused, question marks
- Beat 2: Animated examples (charts, calculations)
- Beat 3: Light bulb, clarity moment
- Beat 4: Application (right or wrong)

---

## Research Requirements

- Concept definition with concrete example
- Real numbers (not hypotheticals)
- Common misconception to address
- Practical application scenario

---

## Example Concepts for This Pattern

**Good Fits**:
- Compound interest
- Margin calls
- Options decay
- Dollar cost averaging
- Cap rates (real estate)
- PMI (mortgages)
- Wash sales
- Opportunity cost

**Poor Fits**:
- Abstract concepts (risk tolerance)
- Subjective topics (best investment)
- Opinions (should you invest)

---

## Quality Metrics

- **Clarity**: Can viewer explain concept after? (90+)
- **Engagement**: Is learning entertaining? (80+)
- **Accuracy**: Is concept correct? (100)
