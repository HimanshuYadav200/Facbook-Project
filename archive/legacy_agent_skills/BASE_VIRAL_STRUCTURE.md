# Base Viral Structure (Universal Foundation)

**Purpose**: Define the viral mechanics that ALL Skulsy scripts must follow, regardless of pattern.

**Applies To**: Every script (what_if, mistake, explainer, success, comparison, timeline, reaction, parable)

---

## The Three Pillars of Viral Content

Every Skulsy script, regardless of pattern, MUST have these three core elements:

---

## PILLAR 1: VIRAL HOOK (0-3 seconds)

**Purpose**: Stop the scroll, create immediate curiosity

### Hook Formula

```
[Subject: Skulsy] + [Action: specific verb] + [Object: specific number/asset/outcome]
```

### Hook Types (Choose ONE per script)

#### Type A: Number Shock
Specific dollar amount or percentage that's surprising.

**Formula**: "What if Skulsy [ACTION] $[AMOUNT] in [ASSET] at [PRICE] in [YEAR]?"

**Examples**:
- "What if Skulsy put $100 into Bitcoin at $1 in 2011?"
- "What if Skulsy bought Amazon stock at $5 in 2001?"
- "Skulsy invested $10,000 at the worst possible time"

**Requirements**:
- Exact dollar amount (not "some money")
- Specific price point (not "cheap")
- Specific year (not "early 2000s")

---

#### Type B: Relatable Mistake
Common financial error viewers recognize from their own experience.

**Formula**: "Skulsy [MISTAKE] at the [WORST/BEST] possible moment"

**Examples**:
- "Skulsy panic-sold at the bottom"
- "Skulsy sold Bitcoin the day before it hit $69,000"
- "Skulsy held cash during the biggest bull run ever"

**Requirements**:
- Action must be relatable (panic-selling, FOMO buying, etc.)
- Timing must be ironic (worst/best moment)
- No explanation in hook (save for body)

---

#### Type C: Unexpected Contrast
Subvert expectations immediately.

**Formula**: "Skulsy [DID X]. It was [OPPOSITE OF EXPECTED]."

**Examples**:
- "Skulsy chose the 'safe' option. It crashed."
- "Skulsy ignored expert advice. He was right."
- "Skulsy diversified perfectly. Still lost everything."

**Requirements**:
- First statement creates expectation
- Second statement subverts it
- Under 15 words total

---

#### Type D: Curiosity Gap
Withhold the critical detail.

**Formula**: "Skulsy made one decision in [YEAR]. [CONSEQUENCE]."

**Examples**:
- "Skulsy made one trade in 2008. Never worked again."
- "Skulsy ignored his broker in 2012. Became a millionaire."
- "Skulsy held for exactly 10 years. Lost it all in year 11."

**Requirements**:
- Withhold what the decision WAS
- State dramatic consequence
- Creates "I need to know" feeling

---

### Hook Quality Checklist

Every hook must pass ALL of these:

- [ ] Contains specific number OR specific mistake
- [ ] Raises clear question in viewer's mind ("What happened?")
- [ ] Under 15 words
- [ ] No vague language ("some", "a lot", "many")
- [ ] No generic assets ("stock" → "Amazon stock")
- [ ] Can be understood in 3 seconds

**Common Hook Mistakes** ❌:
- "What if Skulsy made an investment decision" (too vague)
- "Skulsy bought stock in the 2000s" (not specific enough)
- "What if Skulsy had invested wisely" (no concrete detail)

---

## PILLAR 2: BODY (3-40 seconds)

**Purpose**: Satisfy curiosity with narrative progression, maintain engagement

### Information Density Rule

**New fact every 5-7 seconds** = 6-8 facts total in body.

A "fact" is:
- A specific number
- A decision point
- An event
- A consequence

**Example of proper density**:
```
✅ GOOD (dense):
"He bought at $5. By 2005, it hit $40. He held. By 2008, it crashed to $2. He sold."
(5 facts in ~15 seconds)

❌ BAD (sparse):
"He bought the stock. It went up for a while. Then it went down. He decided to sell."
(2 facts in ~15 seconds)
```

---

### Surprise Cadence

**Pattern break every 10-12 seconds** = 3-4 surprises total.

**Types of Surprises**:

1. **Unexpected Numbers**
   - Setup: "It dropped 10%"
   - Surprise: "Then 30%. Then 80%."

2. **Ironic Timing**
   - Setup: "He held for 10 years"
   - Surprise: "Sold one day before the biggest rally"

3. **Contrast Flips**
   - Setup: "He thought he was diversifying"
   - Surprise: "Everything was tech stocks"

4. **Scale Shifts**
   - Setup: "He made $100"
   - Surprise: "Could've made $100,000"

---

### Timeline Progression

**3+ time anchors showing movement**

**Anchors are NOT info-dumps**. They're story beats.

❌ BAD (feels like a list):
"In 2001, he bought. In 2005, it went up. In 2008, it crashed."

✅ GOOD (natural progression):
"2001. Skulsy bought at $5, convinced tech was dead. By 2005, he was right—it hit $40. Then 2008 came."

**Anchor Spacing Guidelines**:
- Minimum gap: 2 years
- Maximum gap: 10 years
- Sweet spot: 3-5 year gaps

**Phrases for time progression**:
- "By [YEAR], ..."
- "[X] years later, ..."
- "In [YEAR], ..."
- "By the time [EVENT] happened, ..."

---

### Stakes Escalation

Each beat should raise the stakes.

**Progressive Stakes Template**:
1. Small loss/gain → "He lost $1,000"
2. Bigger consequence → "Then $10,000"
3. Life-changing impact → "Then his entire savings"
4. Final blow → "Then his retirement"

**Emotional Escalation**:
- Start: Curious (intensity 0.5)
- Middle: Concerned (intensity 0.7)
- Peak: Regret/Shock (intensity 0.9)
- End: Resolution (intensity 0.8)

---

### Body Structure (Flexible Based on Pattern)

**Setup Beat** (3-8s):
- Establish context
- Introduce decision/scenario
- Set baseline

**Development Beat** (8-25s):
- Events unfold
- Timeline progression
- Surprises accumulate

**Consequence Beat** (25-40s):
- Impact revealed
- Stakes maximized
- Setup for punchline

---

### Body Quality Checklist

- [ ] 6-8 new facts across 37 seconds
- [ ] 3-4 pattern breaks/surprises
- [ ] 3+ timeline anchors
- [ ] Stakes increase, never plateau
- [ ] Every sentence advances story
- [ ] No filler ("he thought about it", "he considered")

---

## PILLAR 3: CLIMAX (40-45 seconds)

**Purpose**: Answer the hook's question, deliver maximum impact

### Climax Types

#### Type 1: The Reveal
Final outcome number that shocks.

**Formula**: "[SUBJECT] became/would've been $[AMOUNT]"

**Examples**:
- "His $100 became $1.2 million"
- "He would've made $5 million. Made $5,000 instead."

---

#### Type 2: The Callback
Reference hook with devastating new context.

**Formula**: Hook element + "But [TWIST]"

**Examples**:
- Hook: "Skulsy bought at the peak"
  Climax: "He sold at the bottom. Perfect timing, wrong direction."

- Hook: "What if Skulsy invested $1,000 in 2010"
  Climax: "He did. Sold in 2011. Before the 10,000% run."

---

#### Type 3: The Twist
Unexpected final detail changes everything.

**Formula**: Expected outcome + "If [ONE THING] had been different"

**Examples**:
- "He would've been a millionaire. If he'd waited one more day."
- "He made the right call. On the wrong stock."

---

#### Type 4: The Understatement
Dark humor via deadpan minimalism.

**Formula**: Massive consequence + minimal reaction

**Examples**:
- "He lost everything. Kept the receipt."
- "His $50,000 became $127. He framed the certificate."
- "He watched Bitcoin hit $69,000. From the sidelines."

---

### Climax Quality Checklist

- [ ] Directly answers hook's implicit question
- [ ] Contains most impactful number of entire script
- [ ] References hook (callback element)
- [ ] Feels inevitable in hindsight
- [ ] Leaves viewer with clear emotional takeaway
- [ ] Under 20 words
- [ ] NO moralizing ("so remember...")
- [ ] NO exclamation marks
- [ ] Deadpan delivery maintained

## Universal Retention Mechanics

These work across ALL patterns:

### The Curiosity Gap
```
Hook creates question
    ↓
Body delays answer (builds tension)
    ↓
Climax delivers answer
```

**Gap duration**: 35-42 seconds optimal

**Maintaining the gap**:
- Don't reveal final number early
- Withhold the "mistake" until Beat 2
- Save biggest consequence for climax

---

### Pattern Breaks (Rhythm)

**Purpose**: Prevent predictability

**Techniques**:

1. **Length Variation**
   - Short. Short. Longer sentence. Short again.

2. **Rhythm Breaks**
   - "He waited. He waited. He sold." (repetition → break)

3. **Direction Flips**
   - "Up 10%. Up 30%. Down 90%." (pattern → reversal)

4. **Expectation Subversions**
   - "He was smart. He was careful. He was wrong."

---

### Emotional Arc (Mathematical)

**Required Intensity Curve**:

```
Hook:         0.5-0.6 (curiosity)
Setup:        0.4-0.6 (neutral)
Development:  0.6-0.8 (building)
Consequence:  0.8-0.9 (peak)
Punchline:    0.9-1.0 (impact)
```

**Rules**:
- Must have at least ONE sentence at 0.9+ (peak)
- Never plateau for 3+ sentences
- Never drop >0.3 in middle of script
- End at 0.7-0.9 (strong resolution)

---

### Specificity Over Generality

**ALWAYS**:

| Vague ❌ | Specific ✅ |
|---------|-----------|
| "He lost money" | "He lost $47,329" |
| "Stock went up" | "Stock hit $156.23" |
| "Early 2000s" | "March 2001" |
| "A lot" | "47%" |
| "Some stocks" | "Tech stocks" |
| "Big gain" | "$2.4 million" |

**Why specificity matters**:
- Concrete details = memorable
- Exact numbers = credible
- Vague language = forgettable

---

## Voice & Tone (Universal)

### Skulsy's Voice Rules

**MUST**:
- ✅ Deadpan narration as default
- ✅ Third person ("He bought. He sold.")
- ✅ Short, punchy sentences
- ✅ Let facts speak (no commentary)
- ✅ Dark humor through understatement
- ✅ Dialogue scenes allowed in `parable` pattern (Skulsy can speak in quotes)
- ✅ Second character allowed in `parable` pattern (advisor, seller, owner)

**NEVER**:
- ❌ Exclamation marks
- ❌ "You should..." (advice to viewer)
- ❌ "Remember..." (moralizing)
- ❌ "This is why..." (explanation)
- ❌ Breaking the fourth wall
- ❌ "Follow us" / "Subscribe" CTAs

---

### Emotional Delivery

**Through narration, NOT through Skulsy**:

❌ BAD:
"Skulsy was devastated when he realized his mistake"

✅ GOOD:
"His $50,000 became $127. He kept the receipt."

The emotion comes from the FACTS, not from describing Skulsy's feelings.

## Integration with Patterns

### How Base + Pattern Work Together

**Base Viral Structure (this file)** defines:
- Hook → Body → Climax framework
- Retention mechanics (surprises, gaps, arcs)
- Voice & tone
- Universal quality standards

**Pattern Skills** (PATTERN_*.md files) define:
- Story structure (what beats to use)
- Emotional journey (curiosity → regret, confusion → insight, etc.)
- Pattern-specific retention triggers
- Visual/narrative style

---

### Pattern Inheritance

Every pattern skill MUST:

1. **Have a hook** from one of the 4 hook types
2. **Build curiosity** in the body
3. **Deliver impact** in the climax
4. **Follow voice rules** (deadpan, third-person)
5. **Maintain density** (fact every 5-7s)

Patterns CAN customize:
- Beat structure (how many beats, what they are)
- Emotional tone (regret, enlightenment, vindication)
- Specific retention triggers
- Timeline anchor placement

Patterns CANNOT skip:
- Hook requirement
- Body density standards
- Climax callback
- Voice consistency

## Examples Across Patterns

### What If Pattern + Base Structure

**Hook** (Number Shock):
"What if Skulsy put $100 into Bitcoin at $1 in 2011?"

**Body** (Facts + Surprises):
- Fact 1: "At $1 per coin, he'd own 100 Bitcoins"
- Surprise 1: "By 2013, Bitcoin hit $1,000"
- Fact 2: "His $100 became $100,000"
- Surprise 2: "He sold everything"
- Timeline: "By 2017, Bitcoin reached $20,000"
- Stakes: "His 100 coins would've been worth $2 million"

**Climax** (Callback):
"Instead, he walked away with $100k. And watched from the sidelines."

---

### Mistake Pattern + Base Structure

**Hook** (Relatable Mistake):
"Skulsy panic-sold at the worst moment"

**Body** (Ironic progression):
- Setup: "March 2020. Markets crashed 30%"
- Decision: "He sold everything"
- Surprise 1: "Two weeks later, markets recovered"
- Surprise 2: "By 2021, they hit all-time highs"
- Stakes: "His portfolio would've doubled"

**Climax** (Understatement):
"He locked in a 30% loss. Permanently."

---

### Explainer Pattern + Base Structure

**Hook** (Curiosity Gap):
"Skulsy didn't know what compound interest meant"

**Body** (Educational progression):
- Confusion: "He put $1,000 in savings. 5% interest"
- Calculation: "He calculated: $50 per year"
- Surprise: "But year 2 wasn't $50. It was $51.25"
- Insight: "Year 3: $53.78. Year 20: $1,326 extra"

**Climax** (Twist):
"Skulsy understood. He moved to a 0.01% account. Wrong lesson."
