In Italy, a bank keeps 500,000 wheels of cheese in its vaults. You see, this is Parmigiano Reggiano, the real Parmesan. Each wheel weighs about 80 pounds, and it has to age for at least 12 months before it can be sold. Often 2 years. But the cows need feeding every single day. So the cheesemakers are stuck. Their money is sitting on a shelf, slowly getting older. Cheese as collateral for a loan? It sounds like a joke. But since the 1950s, a bank called Credito Emiliano has said yes. A cheesemaker brings in young wheels. The bank stores them in giant climate-controlled warehouses, and staff walk the aisles every day, checking for cracks. Each wheel carries its own code, so a stolen one can be traced. And yes, thieves really do steal Parmesan. Then the bank lends up to 80% of the cheese's value. Cash today, for cheese that isn't ready yet. And if the loan isn't paid back? The bank sells the cheese, keeps what it's owed, and hands back the rest. Here's the trick. Most collateral loses value. Cars rust. Machines break. But Parmesan gets better with age, and older wheels sell for more. That's why the bank says it has never lost a single euro on these loans.
