In 1902, a city paid people to kill rats, and ended up with more rats. You see, the French ruled Hanoi back then, and they had just built a shiny new sewer system under their fancy neighborhood. The rats loved it. They climbed up the pipes and popped out of the toilets, right in the middle of a plague scare. So the French hired rat catchers to crawl into the sewers. On one day in June, they killed over 20,000 rats. And it barely made a dent. So officials had a clever idea. Anyone could hunt rats, for 1 cent per rat. And nobody wanted piles of dead rats at city hall, so you only had to bring in the tail. Within weeks, tails were pouring in. Problem solved, right? Then people started spotting rats running around with no tails. The hunters were catching rats, cutting off the tails, and letting them go, so they could make more babies with more tails. Some even smuggled rats in from outside the city. Then health inspectors found farms on the edge of town, raising rats just for their tails. Here's the catch. A tail proved you caught a rat. It never proved the rat was dead. So the bounty was scrapped. The city had paid people to breed rats.
