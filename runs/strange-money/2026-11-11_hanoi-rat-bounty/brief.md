# Brief: hanoi-rat-bounty

- niche: strange-money
- style: explainer-fast
- target length: 0:45-1:10
- topic / angle: seed #2 (B), the Hanoi rat bounty of 1902: French colonial Hanoi paid 1 cent per rat tail; people cut tails off live rats, let them breed, smuggled rats in, and farmed them. The bounty backfired and was cancelled.
- viewer promise (what they understand by the end): why paying for proof (a tail) instead of the result (a dead rat) made people breed rats: the "rat effect" / perverse incentive.
- key facts: 1 cent per rat, tail only; rat catchers' peak day over 20,000; tailless rats; smuggling; rat farms; bounty cancelled. Sources: Wikipedia (Great Hanoi Rat Massacre, built on Michael G. Vann's archive research) + Saigoneer interview with Vann (2024, first published Urbanist Hanoi 2019).
- images: cartoon rats (cute, chunky, no blood or gore); French officials in white colonial suits + pith helmets; Vietnamese rat catchers in conical hats.
