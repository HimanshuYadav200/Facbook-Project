A con man once borrowed $50,000 from Al Capone, did absolutely nothing with it, and got paid for it. You see, this was Victor Lustig, the same man who sold the Eiffel Tower. Now he was in Chicago, and Al Capone was the boss of the Chicago mob. Lustig came to him with a deal. "Give me $50,000, and in 2 months, I'll double it." Capone was suspicious. Cheating him could get you killed. But he handed over the cash. So what did Lustig do with it? Nothing. He locked it away and waited. 2 months later, he went back to Capone with terrible news. The deal had failed, and he had lost everything. "But here's your money back." Capone was stunned. Who gives money back to Al Capone? He was so impressed by this honest man that he handed Lustig $5,000, just to help him get by. And that was the plan all along. Lustig never wanted the $50,000. Stealing from Capone would get him killed. He wanted Capone's trust, and a reward that nobody would ever come looking for. Capone never found out he had been conned. Lustig had robbed the most dangerous man in Chicago, by giving him his money back.
