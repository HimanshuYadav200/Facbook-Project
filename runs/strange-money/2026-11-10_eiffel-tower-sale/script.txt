In 1925, a man sold the Eiffel Tower. You see, Paris was getting tired of it. The tower was built for the 1889 World's Fair, it was rusting, and it needed 60 tons of paint every 7 years. So a con man named Victor Lustig printed fake government letterheads, called himself a deputy director at the ministry, and invited a group of scrap metal dealers to a fancy hotel. He told them the city was secretly tearing the tower down, and selling all 7,300 tons of iron to the highest bidder. It had to stay quiet, or there would be a public outcry. The most famous tower in the world, sold for scrap? It sounds ridiculous. But one dealer, André Poisson, wanted it so badly that he paid for the tower, and then paid Lustig a big bribe on top, just to make sure he won. By the time Poisson realized there was no deal, Lustig was already out of the country with the money. And Poisson? He never went to the police. He was too embarrassed to admit he had bought the Eiffel Tower. So later that year, Lustig came back to Paris and tried to sell it again. This time, someone called the police, and Lustig ran all the way to America.
