For years, Ford shipped new vans to America with back seats, just so it could rip the seats right out. You see, in 1964, America got into a fight with Europe over chicken. Europe slapped tariffs on American chicken, so America hit back with a 25% tax on imported light trucks and vans. People called it the chicken tax. And it never went away. Decades later, Ford was building a little work van called the Transit Connect, in Turkey. Bring it in as a cargo van, and Ford pays 25%. But passenger vehicles only paid 2.5%. So every van left the factory with a back seat, seat belts and rear windows. On paper, it was a passenger wagon. Then the ships landed in Baltimore. Workers pulled out the back seats, swapped the rear windows for metal panels, and the vans were sold as cargo vans. The extra work cost Ford hundreds of dollars per van. The tax it dodged was worth thousands. For years, it worked. Then customs caught on. In 2013, they ruled the vans were cargo vans all along, and said Ford was trying to "manipulate the tariff schedule." Ford fought it all the way to the Supreme Court, which refused to hear the case. In 2024, Ford agreed to pay $365,000,000 to settle, without admitting it did anything wrong. All because of a fight over chicken.
