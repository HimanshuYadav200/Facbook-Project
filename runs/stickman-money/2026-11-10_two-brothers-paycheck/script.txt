Two brothers got their first job on the same day, at the same company, for the same pay. The older brother bought a new car in the very first month. Nice clothes. Dinners out every weekend. The younger brother kept his old bike, and every payday, he put a small part of his pay away before he spent a single dollar. His brother laughed at him. You work just as hard as I do. Why do you live like you're poor? Three years later, the company closed its doors. Both brothers lost their jobs on the same afternoon. The older brother still owed money on the car. His savings lasted two weeks. A month later, the calls from the bank started. The younger brother went home, sat down, and did the math. He could pay his rent and his food for almost a year. He didn't panic. He took his time, and found a better job four months later. One night, the older brother knocked on his door and asked, we earned the same money. How are you okay, and I'm not? The younger brother said, you paid everyone else first. The car dealer. The restaurants. The stores. I just paid myself first. Every month, before anyone else. It never felt like much. But that small part bought me the one thing your car never could. Time. Saving isn't about living poor today. It's about not being trapped tomorrow.
