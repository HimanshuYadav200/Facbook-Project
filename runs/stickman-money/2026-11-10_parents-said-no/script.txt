Your parents never told you how many times they said no to themselves. When you were 7, your mom wore the same grey coat every winter. She said she liked it. She didn't. It just cost less than your new school shoes. Some nights, your dad skipped dinner. He said he wasn't hungry. He was. There was just enough for you, and he made sure you never knew. They talked about a trip to the ocean for years. It never happened. That money quietly became your braces instead. At 16, you wanted a phone like all your friends had. They said yes. You never noticed that your dad kept his old car 3 more years to pay for it. You didn't see any of it. You weren't supposed to. That was the whole point. Now you're grown. You pay your own bills. You know what a winter coat costs. You know what feeding a family costs. And one quiet evening, it finally hits you. They weren't rich. They weren't lucky. They just chose you. Over and over, without a word. Every no they said to themselves was a yes they said to you. So maybe today, call them. Don't talk about money. Don't make it a big speech. Just say thank you, and mean it. And if you can't call them anymore, say it anyway. Somewhere tonight, a mother is wearing an old coat, and smiling.
