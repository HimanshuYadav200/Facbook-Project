A mother gave her daughter $100 and said, spend it however you like. No rules. The girl ran to the mall that same afternoon. A stuffed bear. A bag of candy. Sparkly shoes. A toy she played with only once. By dinner, the money was gone, and she didn't feel a thing. The next Saturday, her mother handed her a pair of garden gloves. This time, I want you to earn $10. The girl laughed. That's easy. Then she saw the garden. Weeds everywhere, tall and stubborn. She pulled them one by one under the hot sun. Her back ached. Her hands got blisters. Twice, she almost quit. By evening, the garden was clean, and her mother placed $10 in her dirty little hand. The girl held it for a long time. That night, her mother found the bill still folded under her pillow. Why don't you spend it? The girl said, because I know what it cost. A whole day. Two blisters. A sore back. Her mother smiled. The $100 cost you nothing, so it left you just as fast. This one cost you a piece of your day, and your sweat. That's why it feels heavier. Years later, the girl got her own paycheck. And before every purchase, she asked one quiet question. How many hours of my life is this worth? Money you earn feels heavier. And heavy money is the money you protect. Somewhere tonight, a child is about to earn her very first dollar.
